Permafrost regions store vast amounts of soil carbon. As they thaw, microbes decompose more organic matter and
release CO<sub>2</sub>, strengthening the permafrost carbon feedback.

Using the Community Earth System Model version 2 (CESM2), **Mun, Lee, Lee, Shin, and Kug** analyzed the permafrost
carbon cycle under both greenhouse warming and subsequent zero-emission pathways. They found that **soil moisture is a
primary regulator**: thaw-induced increases in liquid soil water stimulate plant photosynthesis, but enhance microbial
decomposition even more strongly, shifting ecosystems toward net carbon loss.
